1. Design Scenario – What and Why

This instrument is a meditation tool that uses sound. Its job is to help the user feel calm and mindful. It should feel gentle and safe, not like a normal musical instrument that you have to “play well”.

Every design choice – colours, shapes, sounds – should make the user feel relaxed. The sound should never be sudden or scary. It should change slowly and feel like a soft background texture.

To do this, the interface will be very simple. Big, soft controls will remind the user of breathing. For sound, we will use granular synthesis. This technique cuts a recording into tiny pieces (grains) and plays them back in new ways. It is perfect for making slow, dreamy soundscapes.

2. Project Overview

In Short

GrainMeditate is a web instrument. It uses granular synthesis and sounds from nature and singing bowls. The user moves one main slider to gently shape the sound. There are no sudden changes, only smooth fades. It feels like sculpting soft air.

How It Meets the Scenario

  • Purpose – meditation: It is a background tool. You can leave it on and focus on your breath. You can touch it a little to steer the sound, but you don’t have to.
  • Worth – calm and open to all: Soft, natural colours. No difficult words. No need to know music. At first, you only see one big friendly control. More options are hidden so you are not overwhelmed.

3. Project Framing – What’s In and What’s Out

Framing means deciding the boundaries of the project.

Included (our palette)

  • Platform: Web browser (Web Audio API).
  • Main technique: Granular synthesis (changing grain size, density, pitch, and position in stereo).
  • Sounds: Real recordings of acoustic instruments and nature. No computer-generated waveforms.
  • Interaction: Mouse or touch on one “flow” slider, plus optional hidden controls.
  • Look and feel: Soft gradients, round shapes, slow animations.
  • Audio feel: Always-on ambient sound. No silence unless the user fades the volume down.

Excluded (not in the project)

  • Beats, rhythms, or a step sequencer – they take focus away from calm.
  • Harsh or noisy sounds (no distortion, no bit-crushing).
  • Piano-keyboard or note input – interaction is all smooth and continuous.
  • Visual clutter: no normal knobs, number labels, or complex diagrams.

4. Learning from Other UIs

I studied four browser-based interfaces. Two of them I found on my own (marked with *).

4.1 * “Ambient Chaos” – Purpose & Worth

Ambient Chaos is a very simple webpage. It makes endless ambient sound with only one slider for “intensity”. No words, just a coloured background and one control. This shows that a single control can feel deep if the sound engine is clever.

What I learned: I will also use one main control and map it to several sound features at once (grain density and stereo width).

4.2 Patatap – Character & Information Design

Patatap turns key presses into colourful shapes and short sounds. It has no text instructions. You just press a key and something happens. It feels playful and invites you to explore. However, it works with short, separate taps – the opposite of our slow, smooth meditation tool.

What I learned: I want the same instant feedback without text, but I will make it soft and flowing, not short and jumpy.

4.3 * “Tone Matrix” – Feedback & System State

Tone Matrix is a grid of squares that you turn on and off. It is a step sequencer. The great thing is that you can see the whole system state at once. Each square is both a button and a light. This makes it very easy to understand, with low mental effort.

What I learned: My main “flow” control will also show the state. Its glow will get brighter when the grain density is higher. Other controls will only appear when asked, so the default view stays calm and clear.

4.4 Chrome Music Lab – Spectrogram – Visual Metaphor

The Spectrogram shows sound as a colourful waterfall. It looks beautiful and a bit scientific. You want to make a sound just to see the colours move. This visual metaphor (sound = colour) invites you gently without feeling like a game.

What I learned: I will add a simple particle animation that moves with the audio. It will look like a slow, drifting cloud of light. This helps the user feel that the instrument is alive, even when they are not touching it.
